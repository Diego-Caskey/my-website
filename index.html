THE BEST ZELDA GAME FOR THE GAMEBOY COLOR

The Legend of Zelda: Oracle of Seasons was released for the Game Boy Color in 2001. Developed by Capcom, Oracle of Seasons is a single player adventure RPG that was simutaneously released alongside Oracle of Ages. However, unlike many of Nintendo's dual publications, the Oracle series is considered a two part narrative spanning different lands that feature very distinct game mechanics and story beats. In comparison to a dual release like Pokemon Red & Blue which are narratively and mechanically the same game but each with version exclusive content, players of the Oracle titles will not experience the whole narrative of the Oracle series unless they complete one of the two titles and "Link" their game to the other via a secret passcode generated at the end of a playthrough. With this in mind, this website was designed to effectively brainwash you into forming the opinion that Zelda: Oracle of Seasons specifically is the best Zelda Game released for the Gameboy Color.

Reason 1 : The Intro

Game Boy game intros were known to be around half a minute of cinematic epicness every time you loaded up a game. It sets the mood for your session, especially when your parents only allow you "X" amount of minutes to play. Some intros allude to certain story beats within the game, while others serve the purpose of world building. With this in mind, click on the banner above to watch this game's intro.

Reason 2 : The Sprites

The sprites for this title consisted of 8x8 pixels for most elements, and 8x16 pixels for larger elements, with a maximum of 40 sprites on screen at a time. Each sprite was allowed 3 colors of pixels in addition to transparent pixels, however black pixels usually took one of the three color options by default. In some cases a composition of multiple sprites would act as one "sprite" in order to achieve either more colors or a larger element. For example, when Link rides Dimitri the Dodongo there are actually unique sprites for Link that are trimmed to fit on top of Dimitri's sprite, allowing the player entity to display both Link's colors and Dimitri's colors simutaneously. Despite these limitations, this game features 75+ enemy types, 8 large bosses, and numerous items and NPC's, all of which feature distinct silhouettes and animations. Reason 4 : The Exploration

The land of Holodrum is filled with forests, rivers, mountains, beaches, and fields of secrets and adventure. In addition to certain utility items such as Roc's Feather or the Power Bracelet, the Rod of Seasons allows the player to change the season at will, altering land features in a familiar, yet novel metroidvania exploration system.
In winter rivers freeze over making them traversable, in spring flowers bloom that swallow you whole and shoot you up into the air (obviously), and in fall the trees lose their leaves making them take up less pixels and revealing hidden paths. With logic as innovative as this, progressing through the game is less about strength or skill and more about the player's curiosity, a staple motif in any Zelda title.
